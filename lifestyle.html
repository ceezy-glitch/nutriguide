What can change when weight comes down?

Results vary by person, medication, starting health and how the weight is lost. These are evidence-based examples—not promises.

5–7%

Weight loss

The 2026 ADA Standards state that losing about 5–7% of baseline weight can improve glycemia and other cardiovascular risk factors in people with type 2 diabetes and overweight or obesity.

ADA Standards of Care 2026 ↗
7.3 → 6.6%

A1C in Look AHEAD

In the 1-year Look AHEAD trial, the intensive lifestyle group lost 8.6% of initial weight and mean A1C fell from 7.3% to 6.6%; the comparison group changed from 7.3% to 7.2%.

Look AHEAD trial ↗
↓ BP

Blood pressure

Modest sustained weight loss can improve blood pressure. Food quality matters too: in DASH-Sodium, a DASH pattern plus low sodium substantially lowered systolic pressure compared with a high-sodium control diet.

5–10%

Multiple markers

The CDC notes that even modest weight loss can improve blood pressure, cholesterol and blood sugar. A 5% loss is 10 lb for someone starting at 200 lb.

THE CORE IDEA

Weight loss requires an energy deficit, but there is no single “best” diet.

Guidelines support creating a calorie deficit while choosing an eating pattern that fits the patient’s preferences, medical needs and ability to maintain it. A commonly used clinical starting point is a 500–750 calorie/day deficit, but individualized planning is preferable.

A practical 6-step weight-loss plan

Simple enough to start this week and flexible enough to fit different cultures, budgets and food preferences.

1

Know your starting point

Track 3–7 normal days of food and drinks without trying to be perfect. Look for calorie-dense patterns you can realistically change.

2

Set a modest first goal

A 5–10% weight reduction is a meaningful first target for many people with overweight or obesity.

3

Prioritize fullness

Build meals around vegetables/fruit, lean protein, legumes, high-fiber carbohydrates and water-rich foods.

4

Reduce easy calories

Start with sugary drinks, alcohol, frequent desserts, large restaurant portions, and high-calorie sauces or oils that do not add much fullness.

5

Move consistently

Walking and resistance training support health, fitness and weight maintenance. Start at a level that is safe for you.

6

Adjust from real results

Use 2–4 weeks of weight trend, hunger and adherence—not a single day—to decide whether portions or activity need adjustment.

High-impact food modifications

These swaps target calories and health markers at the same time.

Increase fiber-rich foods

Vegetables, fruit, beans, lentils and whole grains can improve fullness and help with glucose and cholesterol management.

Replace saturated fats

Use olive/canola oil, nuts, seeds and fish more often than butter, high-fat processed meats and other major saturated-fat sources.

Lower sodium where it matters

For blood pressure, compare labels, cook more at home and use herbs, spices, citrus and vinegar for flavor.
